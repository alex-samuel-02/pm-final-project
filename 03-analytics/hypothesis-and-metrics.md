## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** "To mark a stop delivered I tap through three screens. In the rain, at a doorstep, with a package in one hand. I've started just texting my dispatcher instead." (UXR-01, Diego, driver, 3 years.) Another driver adds that every update adds a button and nothing is removed, so Start Route, which he uses 30 times a day, is buried under features he's never touched (UXR-11).
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Drivers barely use most of the app's features: only 3% use the Financial Reporting Suite, and it has the lowest satisfaction score of any feature (1.1 out of 5). Time lost to manual workarounds has risen from about 9 minutes a day to 31, and 4 out of 5 accounts that leave now blame complexity.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** An experienced delivery driver who completes dozens of stops a day. Their goal is to get through every stop quickly with as few taps as possible. The data confirms their friction: the features they use every day are buried under ones they never touch, so they work around the app instead of using it.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Drivers have to dig through features they don't use to reach the few actions they need, so they stop logging their work in the app.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Drivers will log stops in the app instead of texting their dispatcher. That gives coordinators accurate, up-to-date information, so they can finish handoffs and reports. When the reports the buyer paid for actually get done, the product proves its value, which protects renewals and reduces churn caused by complexity.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** The percentage of stops marked delivered in the app within 5 minutes of delivery. If this goes up, drivers are using the app instead of working around it.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Manager satisfaction with Reporting, currently 4.5 out of 5. Managers are the buyers, so hiding features from drivers must not make reporting worse for them. If it drops below 4.3, we stop and investigate.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Run a pilot with a small group of accounts for 8 weeks, which is long enough to see more than one weekly cycle. We scale if in-app stop logging rises by at least 20 percentage points and manager reporting satisfaction stays at 4.3 or above. If logging improves by less than 10 points, we pivot. If the guardrail drops, we stop the pilot.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on drivers telling us they tap through three screens to mark a stop delivered and text their dispatcher instead, and data showing only 3% of drivers use the Financial Reporting Suite (rated 1.1 out of 5) while time lost to workarounds has risen from 9 to 31 minutes a day, I believe that making Start Route and Mark Delivered one tap away and hiding features drivers don't use will result in experienced drivers logging their stops in the app instead of working around it, as measured by a 20-percentage-point increase in stops marked delivered in the app within 5 minutes. I will protect manager satisfaction with Reporting (currently 4.5, not to fall below 4.3) and will make a go/no-go decision after an 8-week pilot with a small group of accounts.
